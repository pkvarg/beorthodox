If there is no pope and no council to give the final word, is everyone free to make the Bible say whatever he likes?

<p class="article-body_highlight">No. Scripture has its own boundaries, its own centre and its own purpose.</p>

## The boundaries

A reader is not free to do anything with the text. The Bible itself sets the limits.

- <strong class="article-body_accent">The text has a meaning.</strong> It was written by an author, in a setting, in a certain kind of writing. We are to handle the word of truth rightly (2 Tim 2:15).
- <strong class="article-body_accent">Scripture explains Scripture.</strong> The Lord opened to his disciples “in all the scriptures the things concerning himself” (Luke 24:27).
- <strong class="article-body_accent">The central things are clear.</strong> “Thy word is a lamp unto my feet” (Ps 119:105). The word is near us, not hidden far away (Deut 30:11–14).
- <strong class="article-body_accent">The Spirit teaches.</strong> The Spirit of truth guides into all truth (John 16:13; 1 Cor 2:12–14).
- <strong class="article-body_accent">The heart matters.</strong> Whoever is willing to do God’s will shall know the teaching (John 7:17). The unstable twist the Scriptures to their own destruction (2 Pet 3:16).
- <strong class="article-body_accent">The church tests.</strong> When one speaks, the others weigh it (1 Cor 14:29), as the Bereans searched the Scriptures daily (Acts 17:11).

## The centre

Boundaries alone are not enough. A person can keep every rule of reading and still miss the point.

“Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me. And ye will not come to me, that ye might have life” (John 5:39–40).

<p class="article-body_highlight">The centre of the Bible is a person. It is Christ.</p>

Moses, the prophets and the psalms all spoke of him (Luke 24:44). God, who spoke in many ways through the prophets, has in these last days spoken to us in his Son (Heb 1:1–2). All things were created through him and for him, and he is the head of the body, the church (Col 1:15–20).

## The governing vision

The Bible also tells us where everything is going. God has made known the mystery of his will: to head up all things in Christ (Eph 1:9–10). And he intends to show his manifold wisdom through the church, according to his eternal purpose (Eph 3:9–11).

Paul sets this against “endless genealogies”, which produce questions rather than God’s own administration, <span class="article-body_accent">his economy</span>, which is in faith (1 Tim 1:4). The word economy is Paul’s own. The early Greek-speaking Christians made it their usual name for God’s plan in Christ, and the Orthodox Church has kept it to this day. In our time Witness Lee brought it back before many of us as the vision that governs the whole Bible, and we gladly credit him for that.

The whole Bible runs along this line. It opens with the tree of life in a garden (Gen 2:9) and closes with the tree of life in a city (Rev 22:2). It opens with man made in God’s image (Gen 1:26) and closes with a bride, a body and a city filled with his glory (Rev 21).

Others have traced the line as covenant, as kingdom, or as the history of salvation. Each of these sees something real. But each of them arrives at the same place: Christ, and a people joined to him.

## A test for every interpretation

This vision gives us a test. Does a teaching hold fast to the Head (Col 2:18–19)? Does it build up the Body in love (Eph 4:15–16)?

<p class="article-body_highlight">Any interpretation that puts a man, an office or a system in the place of Christ fails the test.</p>

That is true when it comes from Rome, and just as true when it comes from a Protestant ministry.

So the church is not a side topic in the Bible. It is where the whole Bible is heading.
