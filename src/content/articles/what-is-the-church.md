Many people today are searching for the Church. But which one are they looking for: the oldest institution, or the original pattern given in Scripture?

We would begin somewhere else.

<p class="article-body_highlight">What matters is not being in the right organization or institution. Our real experience of the church is with <strong>living</strong> people. So the better question is: who is the church to you?</p>

My neighbour Lucia, in our apartment building, is a believer. She is the church to me. My colleague František is the church to me, because I can fellowship and pray with him. Dagmar goes to the Catholic Church, and she is the church to me, because we pray, fellowship and study the Bible together. So are the people from the congregation I go to on Sundays, because we also meet many times during the week to pray and to coordinate.

The church has to be something real, something that shapes my daily life. An organization alone does not do that. Take the Vatican: it may make decisions, proclamations and announcements, yet in practice none of them touches my life.

## The church according to the Bible

<p class="article-body_highlight">First, the church is universal.</p>

“I will build my church” (Matt 16:18). Every born-again child of God belongs to it: those alive today and all who have ever lived.

<p class="article-body_highlight">Second, the church is local.</p>

<span class="article-body_accent">“Tell it unto the church” (Matt 18:17). Can you do this?</span> These words speak of a church you can actually go to. In the Bible, all the regenerated people in a city are the church of that city. There was the church in Rome, the church in Corinth (1 Cor 1:2) and the church in Ephesus. The seven churches of Revelation were the same: Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamos, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia and Laodicea, one church in each city (Rev 1:11).

None of these was an institution or an organization. None was tied to a state, an emperor or a king, and none had anything to do with politics. Each was a body joined to its Head, and the Head is Christ (Col 1:18).
